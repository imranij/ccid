## 2. Structure hypotheses (step 2)

The candidates are **compositions with no structures**. This is the real obstacle, and
everything downstream inherits its uncertainty.

Two independent hypothesis families were built (`a4_prototypes.py`, `a4_hypotheses.py`):

**(A) Prototype substitution**, matched by stoichiometry class:
- **M₄O₇** → ordered-vacancy **defect fluorite** (11 atoms) and **pyrochlore** `Fd-3m`
  (22-atom primitive cell = A₄B₄O₁₄). The pyrochlore builder is general and supports a
  **mixed B-site** (e.g. Al₄[Zr₂Hf₂]O₁₄), which is the physically expected prototype for
  Al₂ZrHfO₇ and which a naive A₂B₂O₇ builder cannot represent. Cation partitions are
  enumerated with the four largest cations placed on the 8-coordinate A-site first.
- **M₄O₆** → rock-salt-ordered **double perovskite** `Fm-3m`.
- **M₃O₄** → **spinel-derived** (2 cations on 8a, 4 on 16d) and **K₂NiF₄** where a cation
  appears twice.
- **M₂O₃** → cubic perovskite `Pm-3m`.

**(B) Random symmetric search** via `pyxtal`, 4 trials per candidate across 12 common
oxide space groups, capped at 30 atoms.

Median 4 hypotheses per candidate (range 2–16); 442 relaxations in total.

### 2.1 A result that matters for the manuscript's Table 12

**The random search beat every hand-picked prototype in 52 of the 66 candidates.** The
median energy spread across a candidate's hypotheses is **0.825 eV/atom**, and for
several of the manuscript's headline candidates it is enormous:

| Candidate | Manuscript's claimed prototype | Spread across hypotheses | Winner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Al₂BaHfO₆ | "Complex oxide (O:4 ratio = 1.50)" | **1.93 eV/atom** | random `P2₁`, not double perovskite |
| Al₂ScLaO₆ | "Complex oxide (O:4 ratio = 1.50)" | **1.91 eV/atom** | random `P2₁/c`-derived, not double perovskite |
| Al₂ZrHfO₇ | "Fluorite/Pyrochlore-like A₂B₂O₇" | 0.23 eV/atom | **pyrochlore** — the manuscript is right here |
| Zr₂HfCaO₇ | "Fluorite/Pyrochlore-like A₂B₂O₇" | **3.82 eV/atom** | pyrochlore, but only after ordering search |

The "double perovskite" reading of Al₂BaHfO₆ and Al₂ScLaO₆ is **structurally wrong**: an
A₂BB′O₆ assignment forces Al onto the 12-coordinate A-site (Al is the only element with
multiplicity 2), which is chemically absurd, and the relaxed energy is ~1.9 eV/atom above
what a random search finds. The manuscript's `structural_prototype` strings should be
removed or replaced with the relaxed space groups reported here.

## 3. Energetics (step 3)

### 3.1 What quantity, and why

No hull was obtainable (§0.A). Instead I computed, entirely within one potential:

```
ΔE_dec = E(candidate) − Σ_i n_i · E(binary oxide i)     [eV/atom]
```

This deliberately **avoids an O₂ reference**, which universal MLIPs reproduce poorly and
which Materials Project only handles via an empirical fitted correction. For the 24
candidates that require a reduced cation (§4.3), the reference is the matching *reduced*
binary oxide (Ti₂O₃ for Ti³⁺, SnO for Sn²⁺), so oxygen balances exactly and no O₂ energy
is ever needed.

### 3.2 The logic — please state it this way in the manuscript

Let `E_cand` be the lowest energy over our finite hypothesis set. Then `E_cand ≥ E_true`,
so our `ΔE_dec` is an **upper bound** on the true decomposition energy. Separately, the
true convex hull lies at or below the binary-oxide tie-line, so the true decomposition
energy is a **lower bound** on the true energy above hull. Therefore:

- **ΔE_dec ≤ 0** → the candidate *is* stable against decomposition into these binary
  oxides. **This is genuine positive evidence.**
- **ΔE_dec > 0** → **inconclusive.** It may be unstable, or our structure search may have
  missed its ground state. It is **not** proof of instability.

### 3.3 Results

53 of 66 candidates have a complete reference set under both potentials.

| Statistic | CHGNet | MACE-MP-0 |
|---|---|---|
| ΔE_dec range (eV/atom) | −0.127 … +0.415 | −0.066 … +0.311 |
| median | +0.086 | +0.100 |
| ΔE_dec ≤ 0 | 5 | 4 |
| ΔE_dec ≤ 0.05 | 19 | — |
| ΔE_dec ≤ 0.10 | 32 | — |

**Cross-potential agreement: MAD 0.030 eV/atom, max 0.219 eV/atom, sign agreement in
52/53.** That is normal uMLIP-to-uMLIP agreement and means the energetics are not an
artefact of one model. (MACE refines the CHGNet-relaxed geometry, so this tests the
*energetics*, not the structure hypothesis.)

**The manuscript's headline candidates** (canonical formulas; full table in
`a4_energetics_*.csv`):

|---|---|---|---|---|

| Zr₂HfSrO₇ | pyrochlore | `P1` | **−0.017** | **−0.002** |
| AlBaLaO₄ | random `sg14` | `P1` | +0.013 | +0.056 |
| BaAlScO₄ | spinel-derived | `C2` | +0.022 | +0.067 |

**Interpretation I would stand behind:** nothing here is wildly unstable. The candidates
sit within roughly 0.1 eV/atom of the binary-oxide tie-line, which for a coarse structure
search and a ~0.03–0.05 eV/atom potential error means **"plausible, not excluded, worth
DFT"** — which is a defensible and genuinely useful claim, and is much stronger than what
the paper currently has. Two candidates (ZrSrCaO₄, Zr₂HfSrO₇) are negative under *both*
potentials, which is real positive evidence.

**The dominant caveat is the structure search, not the potential.** Cross-potential
spread is 0.03 eV/atom; the spread across structure hypotheses for the *same* composition
is 0.825 eV/atom — **27× larger**. Four random trials per candidate is not a converged
search. This is exactly what `a4_dgx_umlip_screen.py` fixes on GPU hardware.

## 4. The descriptor screen, recomputed properly (step 4)

`a4_descriptors.py`, results in `a4_descriptor_screen_all_candidates.csv` and
`a4_descriptor_forensics.json`.

### 4.1 Is the composite score well-defined? No — it is not in the code at all

**`dgx/code/candidate_validation_pack.py` contains no tolerance-factor, no
electronegativity-difference, and no composite-score logic whatsoever.** It is an
unrelated rule-based hazard/review gate (`HIGH_RISK_ELEMENTS`, `validation_gate`,
`priority_expert_review`). I grepped the entire project tree: **no deposited code produces
`candidate_stability_assessment.json`.** The numbers in Table 12 are not reproducible from
the deposited artifacts.

I could nevertheless reconstruct the score empirically. Least squares on the six published
points gives

```
score = 0.4994 + 0.3502·t + 0.0808·Δχ      R² = 0.9998, max residual 5.2×10⁻⁴
```

So the composite score **is** an affine function of the two descriptors — but it is
**ad hoc**: no code defines it, it has no thermodynamic content, the threshold for the
"thermodynamically favorable" band is defined nowhere, and **~85 % of its variance is
carried by the tolerance factor**, which is itself both wrong (§4.2) and undefined for
most of these stoichiometries (§4.4). I recommend deleting the composite score from the
manuscript rather than trying to justify it.

Note this also defeats the manuscript's current defence at line 499, which argues that
*t* is "reported for completeness rather than as the operative stability criterion" while
"the composite score instead weights ionicity via Δχ". That is not what the numbers say:
the composite score is 0.35·*t* + 0.08·Δχ, i.e. it is dominated by *t*.

### 4.2 Δχ reproduces exactly; the tolerance factors do not

**Δχ — reproducible.** All six published values reproduce to 3 d.p. under
`Δχ = χ(O) − ⟨χ_cation⟩` (cation-count-weighted Pauling). That column is fine and can stay.

**t — not reproducible.** I evaluated **twelve** plausible tolerance-factor definitions
against the six published values. The best-matching one is still off by up to **0.065**;
all others by 0.15–1.36. No standard definition using Shannon radii produces 0.277–0.428.

Computed correctly (Shannon radii, A-site CN XII, B-site CN VI, r_O = 1.40 Å), where a
perovskite assignment exists at all:

| Candidate | *t* published | *t* recomputed | Δχ published | Δχ recomputed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Al₂BaHfO₆ | 0.428 | **0.563** | 2.087 | 2.087 |
| Al₂ScLaO₆ | 0.359 | **0.598** | 2.020 | 2.020 |
| Al₂ZrHfO₇ | 0.316 | n/a (non-perovskite) | 1.977 | 1.978 |
| Al₂ZrSiO₇ | 0.277 | n/a | 1.827 | not in deposited pool |
| ScAlHfO₅ | 0.335 | n/a | 2.017 | 2.017 |
| AlZrScO₅ | 0.337 | n/a | 2.007 | 2.007 |

The published tolerance factors appear to be simply **wrong**, not merely
unconventionally defined. The corrected values are also far less alarming — 0.56–0.60
rather than 0.28–0.43 — but they are still outside the perovskite range, which is the
correct conclusion for these compositions.

### 4.3 A problem the reviewers have not yet found: 24 candidates need reduced cations

The generator's `OXIDATION_STATES` table (`composition_cvae_common.py`) allows
**Ti: [3, 4]** and **Sn: [2, 4]**. I tested every candidate against the standard
closed-shell state of each cation:

- **42 of 66** are charge-balanced with standard states.
- **24 of 66 (36 %) are NOT.** They can only be balanced by invoking **Ti³⁺ or Sn²⁺**:
  12 require Ti³⁺, 6 require Sn²⁺, 6 require both.

This is confirmed independently by the stoichiometry: 17 of these candidates are
**oxygen-deficient relative to their fully-oxidised binary oxides** by 0.5–2.0 O per
formula unit, which is the same statement arrived at from a different direction.

**Why this matters.** The design target of the whole paper is a **wide-gap dielectric
insulator**. Ti³⁺ is a d¹ centre and Sn²⁺ a 5s² lone-pair centre; both introduce occupied
mid-gap or shallow states, colour, and electronic conduction. So the charge-balance
filter — the paper's headline chemical-validity gate, and the basis of the 32.9 % validity
figure — is admitting compositions that **directly contradict the design objective**. I
would raise this proactively in the response letter rather than wait for it. Restricting
`OXIDATION_STATES` to closed-shell states is a one-line fix that would change the validity
rate and should be reported honestly if applied.

Every candidate is flagged in `a4_descriptor_screen_all_candidates.csv`
(`requires_reduced_cation`, `redox_note`) and in the LaTeX long table. The DFT generator
flags the same structures as needing spin polarisation.

### 4.4 Is the tolerance factor meaningful here? No — and your reading is correct

The Goldschmidt factor is derived from the geometry of a **corner-sharing BO₆ octahedral
perovskite framework**. It has no meaning for pyrochlore, defect fluorite, weberite,
spinel or K₂NiF₄ frameworks, which have different coordination environments and different
packing constraints. There is no A-site and no B-site to put in the formula.

I computed *t* **only** where a perovskite-derived A/B assignment is defensible (ABO₃ and
A₂BB′O₆). That is **13 of the 66 candidates**. For the other **53** the correct entry is
"n/a", not a number.

**Recommendation:** the manuscript should stop reporting *t* as a stability criterion.
The defensible options are (a) drop it entirely, or (b) report it only for the 13
perovskite-derived candidates, with corrected values, explicitly as a geometric
plausibility indicator and not as thermodynamics. Reporting *t* ≈ 0.3 alongside the phrase
"thermodynamically favorable" is exactly the combination the reviewers objected to, and
they are right.

## 5. Hand-off for the DGX (step 5)

Two documented, tested, command-line scripts, both in `results/`:

### `a4_dgx_umlip_screen.py`
Full-scale GPU uMLIP screen. Supports MACE-MP-0, CHGNet, M3GNet/matgl and SevenNet.
Does what the sandbox could not:
- **hundreds** of pyxtal random trials per candidate across all 230 space groups
  (`--n-random 200`), which is what actually tightens the upper bound;
- with `MP_API_KEY` set, pulls competing phases per chemical system, **re-relaxes them
  with the same potential**, and builds a genuine internally-consistent convex hull
  (`--hull`), giving the E_above_hull the reviewers asked for;
- checkpoints after every relaxation and resumes.

```bash
pip install mace-torch chgnet pyxtal pymatgen mp-api ase
export MP_API_KEY=...
python a4_dgx_umlip_screen.py \
    --candidates composition_cvae_generated_candidates.csv \
    --out results/ --device cuda --calc mace --n-random 200 --hull
```

### `a4_dft_inputs.py`
VASP and Quantum ESPRESSO input generator, **tested and working** in this environment.
- VASP via pymatgen `MPRelaxSet`/`MPStaticSet`, so energies are directly comparable to MP
  formation energies and hull data. Two-step `relax/` (ISIF=3, 520 eV) + `static/`
  protocol with `LWAVE`/`LCHARG` retained for a follow-up `LEPSILON` dielectric run —
  which is what this paper actually wants. POTCARs need a licensed `POTCAR_DIR`
  (`pmg config -p <path> POTCAR_DIR`); `POTCAR.spec` is written meanwhile.
- QE `vc-relax` + `scf` with SSSP-efficiency pseudopotential names, 60/480 Ry.
- **Automatically flags the Ti/Sn structures as spin-polarised** (`ISPIN=2` / `nspin=2`)
  in `MANIFEST.csv`, because the non-spin-polarised default is wrong for them.

```bash
python a4_dft_inputs.py --structures a4_structures/ --out dft/ --code both \
                        --energetics a4_energetics_chgnet.csv --top 12
```

Caveats stated in both scripts' docstrings: cutoffs and k-meshes are sensible but **not
converged**; competing phases must be run at identical settings.

## 7. What I would caution the authors about

1. **Do not upgrade the language.** ΔE_dec ≈ +0.09 eV/atom from a 4-trial structure search
   is *not* "thermodynamically favorable" and *not* "stable". It is "not excluded". The
   two candidates with ΔE_dec < 0 under both potentials are the only ones with genuine
   positive evidence, and even they have no hull distance.
2. **The structure search is the weak link**, by a factor of 27 over the potential error.
   Rerun `a4_dgx_umlip_screen.py` with `--n-random 200` before quoting any of these
   numbers in the paper. My numbers will move — most likely *downwards*.
3. **Table 12 cannot stand.** The tolerance factors are unreproducible and, recomputed,
   different; the composite score exists in no deposited code; *t* is undefined for 53 of
   66 candidates; and one of the six candidates is not in the deposited pool at all.
4. **Raise the Ti³⁺/Sn²⁺ issue yourselves** (§4.3). 36 % of the "valid" candidates are
   charge-balanced only via reduced, open-shell cations that contradict a wide-gap
   dielectric target. A reviewer who notices this after you have defended the validity
   rate will be much harder to answer than one who reads your own disclosure.
5. **13 candidates have no energetics at all** (Nb/Ta, §0.B). Do not let them silently
   disappear from a results table — state the gap.
6. Every number in this report comes from a script in `analysis/a4_umlip/` run on
   the data in `<repo root>/`. Nothing is estimated, interpolated or
   recalled. Where something could not be computed, the cell is blank and labelled.
